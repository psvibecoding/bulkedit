How to Schedule Price Changes on Shopify (Go Live Automatically)

Shopify doesn't let you schedule price changes natively. Your Black Friday prices go live when you remember to log in — not at midnight when the sale actually starts. Here's how to fix that.

Why Shopify doesn't have native price scheduling

Shopify has a "publish date" feature for new products and a discount code scheduler — but neither lets you change the actual price of existing products at a specific time. The product price field has no scheduling mechanism built in.

This means every time-sensitive price change — a flash sale, a BFCM launch, a seasonal markdown — requires you to be online at the exact right moment and manually push the change. For a midnight launch in a different timezone, that means setting an alarm.

For stores running frequent promotions, this is operationally unsustainable.

What scheduled price changes look like in practice

The workflow is: stage your changes now, set a go-live time, walk away. The changes execute at exactly the time you specified — whether you're awake or not.

Step by step

  1. Load your Shopify catalog in Lederly — products appear instantly in a table
  2. Make your price changes inline — click any price cell and type the new value, or apply a bulk rule across a selection
  3. Review the diff — every old price → new price shown before anything is staged
  4. Click Schedule instead of pushing immediately
  5. Set the go-live date and time (with timezone)
  6. Optionally set an auto-revert time — prices restore to original values automatically
  7. Confirm — you'll get an email when the schedule executes

Use cases where scheduling matters

Black Friday / Cyber Monday

BFCM sales often need to go live at midnight or at a specific time coordinated with email campaigns and ads. If your email says "sale starts at 9pm EST" and you're manually pushing prices at 9:12pm, you've already broken the promise to your customers.

Scheduling the change in advance means the sale starts exactly when your marketing says it does.

Flash sales

24-hour or 48-hour sales that need a hard end time. Without scheduling, someone has to manually revert prices at the exact end time — which almost never happens on schedule. With auto-revert, the end is as precise as the start.

Seasonal markdowns

End-of-season clearance that goes live on a specific date. New collection pricing that replaces old pricing automatically. Any change with a known effective date.

Limited product drops

Schedule a product to go ACTIVE at drop time and back to DRAFT if the window closes — without touching the admin manually.

What about Shopify discount codes instead?

Discount codes are not the same as price changes. A discount code requires the customer to know the code and enter it at checkout — it's opt-in. A scheduled price change applies to the listed price on the product page, which affects conversion rates, Google Shopping feeds, and any customer who doesn't know they need a code.

For sale pricing that should be visible to all customers without friction, changing the actual product price is the right approach — and that's what scheduling handles.

Scheduling is available on Growth and Pro plans. Auto-revert requires Pro. Both are free during the current early access period.

Getting notified when a schedule runs

Every scheduled change sends an email confirmation when it executes: which products were updated, what changed, and whether any product encountered an error. You don't need to log in to verify the sale went live — the email tells you.

For auto-revert, you get a second email when the revert executes at the end of the promo.
