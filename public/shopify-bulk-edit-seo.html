How to Bulk Edit Shopify SEO (Titles, Meta Descriptions, URL Handles)

Shopify SEO fields are buried at the bottom of each product page. Optimizing them across a catalog of hundreds of products means hundreds of individual edits. Here's how to do it in one pass.

Which SEO fields can you bulk edit on Shopify?

Shopify gives every product page three SEO-specific fields:

  • Page title — the title shown in search results and browser tabs (separate from the product name)
  • Meta description — the snippet shown under the title in search results
  • URL handle — the slug at the end of the product URL (yourstore.com/products/[handle])

By default, Shopify autofills the page title from the product name and leaves meta description empty. For most catalogs, this means hundreds of products with generic titles and no meta descriptions — a significant SEO gap.

Why this matters for organic traffic

Search engines use the page title as the primary signal for what a page is about. A product named "Blue Wool Pullover" has a page title of "Blue Wool Pullover" — which tells Google very little about intent, category, or audience.

A page title of "Men's Blue Wool Pullover — Merino, Machine Washable | Your Store" is more descriptive, includes category keywords, and differentiates from generic results. Multiplied across hundreds of products, this is the difference between an organic-optimized catalog and one that ranks on product name alone.

Meta descriptions don't directly affect rankings but they affect click-through rate — a compelling description in the search snippet drives more clicks at the same position.

Pattern-based title generation

Instead of writing each title manually, set a title pattern that combines existing product data. For example:

  • {product_name} — {product_type} | {store_name}
  • Buy {product_name} Online — {store_name}
  • {product_name} | Free Shipping on Orders Over €50

Apply the pattern to a selection and Lederly generates the title for each product. Review the results in the diff before pushing — you can spot any products where the pattern produces an awkward result and fix them inline.

Inline meta description editing

For meta descriptions, the most effective approach is to write them product-by-product — they need to reflect the actual product and include a call to action. But you can also use Lederly to identify which products have empty meta descriptions (sort by the field, empty values surface first) and work through them systematically in one session.

URL handle cleanup

URL handles are less frequently edited but matter when: you're migrating from another platform and want to preserve legacy URLs, you're cleaning up auto-generated handles that include numbers or unwanted characters, or you're standardizing handle format across a catalog.

Changing a URL handle changes the product's URL. If the product has backlinks or is indexed in Google, the old URL will 404 unless you set up a redirect. Shopify creates redirects automatically when you change a handle through the admin — Lederly does the same. But always verify redirects are in place before pushing handle changes on indexed pages.

SEO title best practices for Shopify products

Keep titles under 60 characters

Google truncates titles longer than ~60 characters in search results. Anything beyond that is cut off with "…" and may not display your store name or key differentiators. Aim for 50–60 characters for the most predictable display.

Lead with the product keyword

Put the most important keyword at the start of the title, not the end. "Merino Wool Pullover Men's — YourStore" outperforms "YourStore — Men's Merino Wool Pullover" because the keyword appears earlier in the title.

Don't keyword-stuff

A title like "Wool Pullover Sweater Knitwear Jumper Merino Men's Winter" reads as spam to both Google and users. One clear product description plus a brand identifier is the right structure.

Meta description best practices

  • Stay under 155 characters to avoid truncation in search results
  • Include a clear benefit or differentiator — not just a repeat of the title
  • End with a call to action: "Shop now," "Free shipping," "In stock"
  • Include the main keyword naturally — it's bolded in the snippet when it matches the query
